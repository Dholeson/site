How it works

A Gen I save is a raw 32 KB SRAM dump with no container format — every field sits at a fixed offset. The Pokédex is two 19-byte bitfields at 0x25A3 (owned) and 0x25B6 (seen), one bit per species. Money is binary-coded decimal. Names use a bespoke character map that is not ASCII. The checksum at 0x3523 is 255 minus the sum of every byte from 0x2598 to 0x3522, so a single flipped bit anywhere in the main block shows up immediately — which is how you spot a save battery on its way out.

Offsets were taken from Bulbapedia and cross-checked against Rhydon, because the summarised version of that page puts the player name and the dex bitfield at the same offset, which cannot both be true. The parser this page runs on is the same one from my dex project, compiled to plain JavaScript and shipped as-is — it is pure byte arithmetic, so it does not care whether it is running on a server or in your tab.

Three completion figures are shown rather than one, because Gen I has three honest answers: what one cartridge can reach alone, what trading adds, and the full 151 — which needs a Mew that was never legitimately distributed on a US cart.
