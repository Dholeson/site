Chiptune Sequencer

Six tracks and sixteen steps, and every sound is generated as it plays. A pattern is already loaded: press Play.

132 BPM

Tap or click a square to switch that step on or off. The darker squares mark each beat, every four steps. On a narrow screen each track's sixteen steps wrap onto two rows of eight.

How it works

The three melodic tracks are C5, G4 and E4, a spread of the same chord, so anything you draw stays consonant. Bass is a triangle wave on C3, two octaves below the lead. Snare and hat are the same one-second buffer of white noise, run through a highpass filter at different cutoffs and cut short by different envelopes.

The interesting part is the timing. Driving a sequencer directly from setInterval drifts, because timer callbacks jitter under load. Instead the timer only looks ahead: it wakes every 25 ms and schedules any notes falling in the next 100 ms straight onto the AudioContext clock, which is sample-accurate. The notes are queued ahead of time, so the playhead you see is delayed on purpose to line up with the audio you are hearing.
