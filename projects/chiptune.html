Chiptune Sequencer

Six tracks, sixteen steps, and not a single audio sample — every sound is an oscillator built at runtime.

Click any square to toggle it. A pattern is loaded already — press Play. Bars are marked every four steps.

How it works

The three melodic rows are C5, G4, and E4 — a spread of the same chord, so anything you draw stays consonant. Bass is a triangle wave an octave and a half down. Snare and hat are the same one-second buffer of white noise, run through a highpass filter at different cutoffs and cut short by different envelopes.

The interesting part is the timing. Driving a sequencer directly from setInterval drifts, because timer callbacks jitter under load. Instead the timer only looks ahead: it wakes every 25 ms and schedules any notes falling in the next 100 ms straight onto the AudioContext clock, which is sample-accurate. The playhead you see is deliberately delayed to match the audio you are hearing rather than the notes already queued.
