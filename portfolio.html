Controller Drift Tester

Plug in a gamepad and get a measured number for how far its sticks have wandered.

Problem

Used controllers get sold as "no drift" based on someone wiggling the stick and shrugging. I wanted a reading I could put in a product listing.

What I built

  • Live plot of both analogue sticks via the browser Gamepad API, drawn on canvas.
  • A two-second rest measurement: let go of the sticks and it samples the resting offset and jitter, then gives a verdict against a typical in-game deadzone.
  • Used for real — every controller in the shop is checked with it before listing.

Chiptune Sequencer

A six-track step sequencer where every sound is synthesised at runtime.

Problem

Make retro-console-style music in the browser without samples or an audio library — just oscillators and a noise buffer.

The interesting part

  • Driving a sequencer straight off setInterval drifts audibly, because timers aren't sample-accurate. Instead a short timer acts as a lookahead, scheduling upcoming notes directly on the AudioContext clock, which is.
  • Square, triangle and noise voices; adjustable tempo, waveform and volume.

The Shop: storefront and checkout

A store for refurbished retro consoles, on a static site with one serverless function.

Problem

Take card payments without running a server, and without letting the browser decide what anything costs.

Decisions worth looking at

  • The browser never sends a price. The cart sends product ids and quantities only; a Cloudflare Pages Function prices the order from the catalogue and creates the Stripe Checkout session.
  • One source of truth. The same catalogue file renders the store and prices the checkout, so they can't disagree.
  • Money is integer cents, never floats.
  • Tested where it matters: 16 tests on the pricing path — including one proving a client-supplied price is ignored, plus stock limits and malformed or hostile carts.
  • Strict Content Security Policy with no inline scripts anywhere on the site.
